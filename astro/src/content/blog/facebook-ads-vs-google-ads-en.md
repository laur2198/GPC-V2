Almost every entrepreneur who wants to advertise online lands in the same dilemma: do I put money into Google or into Facebook? The short answer is that the question is wrong. The two platforms don't do the same job, and the choice isn't about which one is "better" — it's about **where your customer is at the moment they make the decision**.

In this article I'll explain the real difference between the two, when each one makes sense depending on your type of business, and the minimum budget you need so you don't throw money away.

## The difference nobody spells out clearly

Google Ads answers **existing demand**. Someone is already searching "plumber Brașov" or "PVC window fitting" — they need it now, they type it into Google, and you show up in front of them at exactly that moment. Purchase intent is high, so conversion rates tend to be better too.

Facebook Ads (and Instagram, through the same Meta platform) **creates demand**. Nobody opens Facebook to search for a product. You interrupt their scroll with a well-targeted offer. It's ideal when your product is new, when people don't know they need it, or when you sell something visual — clothing, food, beauty services, events.

In short: **Google catches people who are looking for you; Facebook finds people who aren't looking for you yet.**

## When to choose Google Ads

Google Ads makes sense when:

- You sell a service people **actively search for** when they have a problem: installations, repairs, medical services, lawyers, auto service, moving.
- You work locally and want customers from your area — "near me" searches are pure gold.
- You need customers **now**, not in three months. Google brings leads faster because it catches intent in the moment.
- Facebook competition doesn't help you because the product isn't impulsive (nobody books a dental procedure on impulse from a reel).

The downside: cost per click in competitive niches (legal, medical, financial) can be significantly higher than on Facebook.

## When to choose Facebook / Meta Ads

Meta Ads makes sense when:

- Your product is **visual or impulsive**: fashion, food, cosmetics, décor, events.
- You want to build awareness for a new brand nobody is searching for yet.
- You have a clearly defined demographic audience (age, location, interests) you can target precisely.
- You want high impression volume at low cost — in Romania, CPC and CPM on Meta remain among the most affordable, and the active audience exceeds 12 million users.

The downside: people aren't in "buying mode", so you need a strong message and image to stop the scroll, plus several touchpoints before conversion.

## Examples by industry

**Restaurant / café (Brașov):** Meta wins. People don't search "new restaurant" on Google, but they react to a good plate shot and a weekend offer. Google only stays useful for "restaurant near me".

**Dental clinic:** Google wins. Someone in pain searches immediately. Facebook helps secondarily, for awareness and reviews.

**Clothing / accessories store:** Meta wins clearly. Visual product, impulse buying, strong retargeting on people who visited the site.

**PVC carpentry / construction firm:** Google primary (high intent, high customer value), Meta secondary to stay in the mind of those still deciding.

**Event / concert:** Meta wins. Interest targeting + retargeting on people who engaged.

See the pattern: there's no universal winner. **The type of demand dictates the platform.**

## Why a small budget "doesn't work"

The most common mistake isn't the platform choice — it's a budget that's too fragmented. Below a certain threshold, the algorithms don't get enough data to optimize. As a realistic benchmark in Romania:

- **Minimum media budget** for relevant results: ~300–500 EUR/month on a single platform. Below that, there's too little data for proper optimization.
- If you split 200 EUR across Google *and* Facebook at once from the start, you usually succeed nowhere. Better to pick one, take it to a result, then expand.

The practical rule: **one platform, a decent budget, until the first clear result.** Then scale or add the second.

## The right answer for most businesses

If people are already searching for what you sell — **start with Google.**
If you need to spark the desire — **start with Meta.**
If you have budget for both and want a complete system — **Meta to generate demand, Google to catch it at the end** — but only after one of them works on its own.

The most expensive mistake is starting both at once, on a small budget, without a landing page that turns the click into a customer. Because bringing traffic is pointless if the page they land on doesn't sell — but that's a topic for a separate article.
