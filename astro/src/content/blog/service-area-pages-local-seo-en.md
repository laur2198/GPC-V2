If your business works across several towns or regions, you have a problem a plain brochure website won't solve: the customer in the next city isn't searching for your name. They search for what you sell, plus their city. If you don't have a page answering exactly that search, Google shows someone else.

The classic fix is service-area pages. The wrong fix, and the most common one, is cloned service-area pages. Below I show the difference on a real project: [ioana-balan.ro](https://www.ioana-balan.ro/), the website of a Bucharest folk singer with a full band who performs within roughly 300 km.

## Why area pages, not just "we cover the whole country"

A line like "we travel nationwide" is true, but it ranks for nothing. Google answers local searches with pages that are about that place. One paragraph listing 20 county names is not a page about any of them.

For Ioana Bălan I built a hub, the **service areas** page, and **20 local pages**, one for each county where the band plays regularly, from Ilfov and Prahova to Constanța, Dolj and Mehedinți.

## What separates a good area page from a cloned one

The most common mistake is the template with the city swapped: same text, different name in the title. Google recognises the pattern, treats it as thin content and indexes one or two pages out of twenty. The rest exist but bring nothing.

A good area page answers the questions customers in that area actually have. In our case, couples in a given county want to know three things:

1. **Has the band played here before?** Each page lists the towns and venues in that county where the band has already performed. It is local social proof and, at the same time, unique content no competitor can copy.
2. **How much is travel?** The rule is public and the same everywhere, a per-kilometre rate for the round trip from Bucharest, but each page says what it means in practice for that county.
3. **Is accommodation needed?** This is where pages genuinely differ. Some counties are close enough for the team to drive back the same night. Others require a room: Constanța, for example, or Brașov for evening events. Couples learn this before they ask for a quote.

The general rule, for any industry: **if you can delete the city name from a page and the text stays just as true, the page isn't about that city.**

## The structure I used

- **The area hub**: explains the pricing rule once and links to every county, ordered by distance.
- **The county page**: title and meta description built on the local search, the area-specific details, links to the packages and to the event pages (wedding, christening, corporate).
- **Internal links in both directions**: from service pages to areas, and from areas back to services and packages. Otherwise area pages end up orphaned and Google gives them no weight.
- **One CTA, the same everywhere**: WhatsApp booking with a pre-filled message. The area page is not a destination, it is a shortcut to a conversation.

## The AEO part: short answers to direct questions

More and more searches get a direct answer, in Google's results or in an AI assistant, with no click. To be the source of that answer, the question has to exist on your site, phrased the way the customer asks it, with the answer in the first sentence or two.

On ioana-balan.ro, the questions about price, areas, deposit and contract get direct answers on the homepage and on a dedicated FAQ page. The answer about areas, for example, names the cities where the band plays most often and the travel radius, then links to the pricing rule.

## Public pricing is part of the local strategy

The competitor analysis showed that only about 37% of Romanian wedding bands display their prices. For a premium service, that is an opportunity. A customer who sees the price and what it includes gets in touch already qualified. People looking for something cheap stop taking up everyone's time.

Area pages and the packages page work together: the area says "we can come to you, and here is what travel costs", the package says "this is what you get". The customer reaches WhatsApp with all the information they need.

## Does this apply to your business?

Yes, if two conditions hold: you actually work in several areas, and you have something specific to say about each one. A few examples:

- **Construction, modular homes, windows and doors**: completed projects in the area, delivery and installation times, local permits.
- **Event services**: venues you've worked at, travel costs, logistics.
- **Home services**: areas covered, response time, call-out fee.

If you have nothing specific to say about an area, don't build a page for it. Five real area pages beat forty cloned ones.
