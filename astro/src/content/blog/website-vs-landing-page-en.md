"I want a website." It's one of the most common requests any web company gets — and almost every time it hides a confusion. Because "website" can mean two very different things, with different purposes, different costs, and different results: a brochure website or a landing page.

If you choose wrong, you either pay a lot for something that brings you no clients, or you cut costs on something that would have helped. Let's clear it up, so you know exactly what to ask for.

## What each one is, in short

**The brochure website** is your digital "headquarters". It has several pages — home, about, services, contact, maybe a blog — and its purpose is to represent you fully: who you are, what you do, why someone should trust you. It's where someone lands when they look you up by name or want to check you out.

**The landing page** is a single page, built for a single action. It has no menu to wander you around. It has one goal: to convince the visitor who came from an ad or a campaign to do exactly one thing — call, fill in, sign up, buy.

The essential difference: **the website represents you; the landing page converts.**

## When you need a brochure website

You need a brochure website when:

- You want a stable, credible presence that people find when they search for you on Google or by name.
- You have several services or products that each deserve to be explained.
- You're building long-term trust — reviews, portfolio, the company story.
- You want to invest in SEO and content (a blog) to attract organic traffic over time.

It's the foundation. Almost any serious business needs one at some point, because it's where people check you out before they buy — even if they discovered you somewhere else.

## When you need a landing page

You need a landing page when:

- You're starting an ad campaign (Google or Facebook) and want the paid traffic to have a destination that actually converts.
- You're promoting a specific offer, product, or event and want all the attention on it.
- You want to measure the result clearly: how many arrived, how many acted.
- You need speed — a landing page is made faster and cheaper than a full website.

The golden rule: **if you're paying for ads, you need a landing page.** Sending paid traffic to the site's homepage is one of the most expensive mistakes — the person lands in a general place, has to search for what interests them, and most of the time, leaves. A page dedicated to the offer converts the same traffic far better.

## The classic mistake: pretty site, zero clients

Many companies pay a few thousand lei for a good-looking brochure website and expect it to bring clients on its own. It doesn't happen. A brochure website is passive — it sits there and waits to be found. Without traffic (from SEO, ads, or social), nobody sees how pretty it is.

Conversely, others start ads with no page prepared and send people to the wrong place. Money on clicks, zero conversions.

The truth is the two work best together: the website gives you credibility and long-term presence, the landing pages turn your campaigns into clients. It's not "either-or", it's a question of order and purpose.

## What to choose, depending on where you are

**Just starting out with a limited budget:** if the immediate goal is to bring clients through ads, a good landing page beats a mediocre website. Start with the page that converts, add the full website when you can.

**You already have clients and want credibility:** invest in the brochure website. It's your digital headquarters and the foundation for SEO.

**You're advertising actively:** you need both — a site for presence, dedicated landing pages for each important campaign.

**You want long-term organic traffic:** a brochure website with a blog, SEO-optimized. Landing pages alone don't bring organic traffic.

## Conclusion

Don't ask for "a website" — ask for the right tool for your goal. If you want to represent yourself and be found, it's a brochure website. If you want to turn ads into clients, it's a landing page. Most of the time, a growing business ends up needing both — but knowing from the start which one solves your problem right now saves you wasted money and lost months.
